# Interviewing the project owner

The craft commands exist to draw out what a codebase can't know: who the product is for, what it believes,
how it sounds, what good looks like. Models fill gaps with the most probable answer, which is the generic
one. These rules keep the answers the owner's own.

## Ask well

- **Small batches.** Ask two to four related questions at a time, in plain conversation. Wait for answers
  before moving on.
- **Concrete prompts beat blank ones.** People react to examples more easily than they invent from scratch.
  Offer two or three contrasting options to react to ("closer to a quiet tool, or a lively companion?"),
  then ask what's wrong with each.
- **Push past the generic.** "Clean", "modern", "simple", "intuitive", "user-friendly" and "premium" describe
  everything, so they describe nothing. When you hear one, ask what it would rule out, or for a product that
  gets it right and why.
- **Ask for evidence.** Real customer quotes, support tickets, screenshots, competitor sites, brand
  guidelines, photos of the world the users live in. Read anything they share before asking more.
- **Ask what they've noticed.** What do users do, avoid or complain about that they wouldn't put in a
  survey? What do the best products in the space do that others miss?
- **Find the refusals.** What would embarrass them? What does every competitor do that they won't?
- **Use the structured question tool only for genuine choices** between known options. Open questions go in
  plain text.

## Write it down faithfully

- Use the owner's words where you can. Don't upgrade their language into marketing copy.
- Never fill a gap with your own assumption. Record it under **Open questions** instead.
- Show the draft before saving, and ask what's wrong or missing. Expect a second pass.
- Keep each document short enough that agents will actually read it: specific beats complete.

## Know when to stop

The goal is enough intent to make good decisions, not a perfect document. When answers start repeating, or
the owner wants to move on, save what you have, list the open questions, and say what's still worth
coming back to.
